The craft

From a thought to a treasure.

Keep scrolling to watch a Tyldesley ring come to life — from the first pencil line to the final polish.

01

The sketch

Every piece starts on paper — pencil, compass and a conversation about you.

02

Painted by hand

Caitlin paints three designs in watercolour. You choose the one that feels like yours.

03

To the millimetre

Angles, stone sizes and the spaces between them — the drawing becomes a plan that can actually be built.

04

Modelled in wax

The design takes shape in wax: the exact form your ring will have.

05

Cast in gold

Molten metal takes the place of the wax. Silver, gold or platinum — your choice.

06

Set by hand

Your stone is set claw by claw by one of Caitlin's two master goldsmiths.

07

Polished. Yours.

Polished to a mirror finish. Made once — and never again.

Remakes

Old gold. New life.

“I absolutely love taking the old and turning it into something new. Jewellery is supposed to last a lifetime, and I get to give it new life — worn in new ways, by new owners, with double the meaning.”

Old gold can't always be reused — it's alloyed with other metals we can't always identify. Cast pieces are hard to rework, so that metal is sold and credited towards your new piece.

Meet Caitlin

“I get my inspiration from you.”

Every person is different, so every piece is different — and a challenge. Caitlin creates custom, once-off pieces, designed for you as an individual. Her favourite part? Meeting the people she makes for, and getting to know them through their jewellery.

  1. WindhoekGrew up on a smallholding outside the city — chickens, geese, horses and a LOT of snakes. Her dad was known as the snake man.
  2. 2003 · Grabouw & PaarlMoved to South Africa and took Jewellery Design as a school art subject. The passion took off.
  3. Cape Peninsula University of TechnologyStudied the art further while working in boutique jewellery stores.
  4. 18 years at the benchA year at one of South Africa's top jewellery companies, then Tyldesley Jewellers — working with two incredible goldsmiths.
Her favourite

“Anything cut in an emerald cut. I love the lines and the way the light shines through the stone. My dream piece would be an emerald, cut in the emerald shape, set in yellow gold.”

Yes — that's the ring at the top of this page.
